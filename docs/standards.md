# Standards for school crossings

What "good" looks like, internationally and under Greek rules, and how each source feeds the
project's rating. Links are in [sources.md](sources.md). Items marked **verify** were not checked
against the primary legal text and should be confirmed before they are quoted to an authority.

## The short version

International guidance agrees on five things for a crossing children use:

1. **Slow traffic.** 30 km/h or less where children and vehicles mix.
2. **Speed that is built in, not just signed.** Raised crossings, narrowings, short crossing distances.
3. **Children can see and be seen.** No parked cars or clutter hiding a child at the kerb.
4. **Everyone can use it.** Kerb ramps, tactile paving and a clear footway on both sides.
5. **It stays that way.** Paint, signs and ramps are maintained, and someone is answerable for it.

## International references

| Source | What it sets | How this project uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **iRAP Star Rating for Schools (SR4S)** | Evidence-based 1 to 5 star rating of pedestrian risk around schools. The global target is 3 stars or better. Inputs include operating speed, crossing facility and its quality, number of lanes, sidewalks, median type and traffic flow. Used in 63 countries. | Our A to F grade maps onto 5 to 1 stars and takes "3 stars is the minimum" as the pass mark. Our audit is a citizen checklist, **not** an iRAP rating. A formal SR4S assessment of the worst sites is a later step (see roadmap). |
| **Stockholm Declaration (2020)** | Governments resolved to mandate a maximum of 30 km/h where vulnerable road users and vehicles mix frequently. | The 30 km/h threshold in the speed criterion. |
| **GDCI / NACTO, Designing Streets for Kids and Global Street Design Guide** | Design guidance for streets used by children: raised crossings at sidewalk level, vertical speed control set back 5 to 10 m from the crossing, short crossings, school zones. | Criteria for raised crossings, calming near the crossing and number of lanes crossed. |
| **Clean Cities Campaign, Streets for Kids** | European campaign for school streets (streets closed to through traffic at school times). Its 2025 ranking scores 36 cities on school streets, 30 km/h coverage and protected cycle lanes. Goal: every street in front of a school is a school street by 2030. | The school street criterion, and a ready-made European benchmark and network to join. |
| **WHO speed management guidance** | Speed is the key risk factor for pedestrian death and injury; supports 30 km/h in school zones. | Weighting: the street and speed section carries the most points (35 of 100). |

## Greek framework

| Rule | What it covers | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| **Ν. 5209/2025, new ΚΟΚ** (ΦΕΚ Α΄ 100/13.6.2025), άρθρο 24 | Default limit of 30 km/h in built-up areas unless signed otherwise. 50 km/h remains on one-way streets with two or more marked lanes and some other categories. Reported in force from 1 January 2026 (earlier press reports said 1 November 2025; **verify** the date). | Most streets outside schools are now legally 30 km/h. The audit records what is signed; observed speeds are a later addition. A claimed 20 km/h limit around schools appeared in press coverage of the draft law; **verify** before using it. |
| **ΥΑ ΔΜΕΟ/ο/3050/2013** (ΦΕΚ Β΄ 2302/16.9.2013) | Technical guidelines for traffic interventions around school complexes and areas with heavy pedestrian activity: traffic-calmed zones, a raised crossing near the school entrance, widened footways at entrances at the expense of parking, a guide path for children, signs and markings around the school block. | **The key national standard.** It is what a municipality's own engineers are supposed to apply, so every request to an authority should cite it. |
| **ΥΑ ΥΠΕΝ/ΔΜΕΑΑΠ/124964/1561/2022** (ΦΕΚ Β΄ 6213/7.12.2022), which replaced ΥΑ 52907/2009 (ΦΕΚ Β΄ 2621); amended by ΥΠΕΝ/ΓΓΧΣΑΠ/61667/95 (ΦΕΚ Β΄ 2859/6.6.2025) | Accessibility of public pedestrian space: free walking zone, kerb ramps, tactile guide for blind people. Supported by the "Σχεδιάζοντας για όλους" design guidelines. | Basis of the accessibility section. Exact dimensions (clear width, ramp slope) must be taken from the current text; the audit's 1.5 m clear width is our working figure, **verify**. |
| **Σχέδιο Αστικής Προσβασιμότητας (ΣΑΠ)**, ΥΑ ΥΠΕΝ/ΔΜΕΑΑΠ/110088/887 (ΦΕΚ Β΄ 5553/2021) | Specifications for each municipality's urban accessibility plan. | A municipality's ΣΑΠ should already list routes to schools. Ask for it and hold them to it. |
| **Σχολικοί τροχονόμοι**: ΚΟΚ άρθρο 45 (ν. 2696/1999), ν. 2817/2000 άρθρο 14 παρ. 45, ΚΥΑ 32174/2025 (ΦΕΚ Β΄ 3350/1.7.2025) | Volunteer school crossing guards: one per primary school in municipalities up to 10,000 residents, up to two in larger ones. They stop traffic at a marked crossing (not at a junction). Compensation is paid through the school committees or the municipality. | The "protection at school hours" criterion. Whether a school has its guard is a simple, checkable accountability question for the school and the municipality. |

## Programmes already running in Greece

Worth knowing because they are potential allies, and because they raise the maintenance question
this project exists to ask.

- **"Smart" school crossings (ΤΕΕ, Ελλάδα 2.0).** A contract signed in February 2025 covers 843
  crossings outside schools in 107 municipalities for €41.16 million before VAT (about €48,800
  per crossing), with LED road studs and signs triggered by a pedestrian, solar power, sensors and
  a central platform. Warranty and technical support run for 2 years after acceptance.
  **Open question for every municipality that receives one: who maintains it in year 3, and from which budget line?**
- **Municipality of Athens pilot (July 2025).** Trial of anti-slip surfacing ahead of school
  crossings to make drivers slow down, with expansion to other municipal communities announced.
- **Hellas Direct, Safe Roads Project.** A private initiative that repaints faded school
  crosswalks on request; by December 2025 it reported more than 1,800 crossings in over 110
  municipalities.

## What the standards do not give you

None of these documents says how often a crossing must be inspected or repainted, or publishes
the condition of each one. That gap between a design standard and a kept-up crossing is the
project's reason to exist: see [accountability.md](accountability.md).

# Promotion

## Message

One sentence: **"Κάθε παιδί να περνάει απέναντι με ασφάλεια."**
One number per municipality: **"Χ στις Ψ σχολικές διαβάσεις κάτω από τη βάση."**
One ask: **"Διορθώστε τις, και πείτε μας πότε."**

Avoid the word "ατύχημα". Keep the tone factual: the grade and the photo do the arguing.

## Audiences and what each one gets

| Audience | Hook | Material |
|---|---|---|
| Parents | "Τι βαθμό παίρνει η διάβαση του σχολείου σας;" | Link straight to their school on the map; a 3-minute audit they can do at pick-up |
| School heads and teachers | A ready lesson: pupils audit their own crossing | One-page classroom guide; the audit as a road-safety activity |
| Municipal councillors | A report card for their community, with the cheap fixes listed first | Two-page PDF per municipality |
| Local press | A ranked list, a worst-crossing photo, a days-without-fix counter | Press note plus three images |
| Engineers and planners | The method and open data | `docs/` and the GeoJSON |

## Tactics that fit a small team

1. **School report cards.** One page per school: its crossings, grades, what is missing, who is
   responsible. Printed and pinned on the parents' noticeboard with the school's permission.
2. **Before and after.** Every fix gets a paired photo. This is the content that travels, and it
   credits the service that did the work.
3. **Municipal league table**, published once a year: share of school crossings at C or better,
   and median days to fix. Rank only municipalities with enough audits to be fair.
4. **"Days without a fix" counter** on each reported crossing. Simple, hard to argue with.
5. **Audit walks.** A Saturday morning, ten parents, one neighbourhood, every crossing done in
   two hours. Invite the local councillor and a journalist to walk along.
6. **Pupils as auditors.** Upper primary and gymnasio classes audit their own route. Children
   presenting their findings to the council is the strongest image the project can produce.
7. **Candidate pledges** before municipal elections: "every school crossing at C or better within
   two years", signed and published.

## Calendar hooks

| Moment | Use |
|---|---|
| School start, September | Launch of the yearly audit round; back-to-school coverage is guaranteed |
| European Mobility Week, 16 to 22 September | Audit walks, school street trials |
| Streets for Kids actions (spring and autumn, Clean Cities Campaign) | Join the Europe-wide action with a Greek event |
| World Day of Remembrance for Road Traffic Victims, third Sunday of November | Sober release of the worst-crossings list, with victims' organisations |
| UN Global Road Safety Week (May, in the years it is held) | Annual report and league table |

## Channels

- A short, memorable domain pointing to the map.
- Instagram and Facebook groups of parents' associations: this is where school communities
  actually talk. One post format, repeated: photo, grade, who is responsible, days waiting.
- Local news sites per municipality rather than national media at first.
- A monthly email of fixes and new audits for ambassadors and partners.

## Launch sequence

1. Quiet pilot: audit one community, file requests, get one fix.
2. Brief partners privately and ask two or three to co-sign the launch.
3. Public launch with the pilot's results, not with a promise.
4. Open audits to everyone, with the pilot schools as the worked example.
